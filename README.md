# Warehouse Demand & Product Performance — 2018–2019 Analytical Decision Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The analysis identifies a clear deterioration in business volume between 2018 and 2019.

The most important finding is that **the business did not experience a meaningful reduction in the number of orders; it experienced a substantial reduction in the volume generated by those orders**.

| KPI              |       2019 |    YoY Change |
| ---------------- | ---------: | ------------: |
| Total Demand     | 649M units |    **-37.5%** |
| Total Orders     |       ~25K |     **-0.3%** |
| Demand per Order | ~26K units |    **-37.3%** |
| Revenue          |  $225.64bn |    **-37.0%** |
| Value per Unit   |    $370.06 |     **+3.4%** |
| Products         |         20 | **No change** |

The evidence therefore points decisively toward a **volume contraction rather than an order-count or pricing problem**.

The product analysis explains the contraction. In 2018, the top five products generated approximately **61M–82M units each**. In 2019, the top five generated only **40M–49M units each**.

Two major historical volume drivers experienced particularly severe declines:

* **Whathtype:** ~82M → ~27M units
* **Streamer:** ~64M → ~26M units

Together, these represent approximately **93M units of lost demand**.

At the same time, products entering the 2019 Top 5 did not generate enough volume to compensate for the lost demand.

### Core conclusion

> **The 37.3% decline in demand per order is driven by a substantial contraction in product-level demand, particularly among historically high-volume products. Order volume remained broadly stable, but the amount of product being generated across those orders fell sharply, causing the average demand per order to decline.**

The strategic priority is therefore **volume recovery**, not simply increasing order count or reducing prices.

---

# 2. Business Problem

The central business problem is the sharp decline in demand and revenue despite relatively stable order activity.

This is particularly concerning because increasing order volume alone is unlikely to solve the problem.

The business needs to understand:

* Which products are responsible for the lost volume?
* Which categories are driving the decline?
* Which warehouses are experiencing the greatest impact?
* When did the decline occur?
* How can the lost volume be recovered?

The dashboard analysis provides sufficient evidence to answer the first part of this problem and establish a clear direction for management action.

---

# 3. Overall Performance Deterioration

The headline performance indicators show a major decline.

### Demand

Total demand declined by:

**37.5% YoY**

This represents a substantial contraction in the amount of product being moved through the business.

### Revenue

Revenue declined by:

**37.0% YoY**

Revenue therefore broadly follows the demand decline.

This is important because it indicates that the revenue problem is predominantly associated with **volume deterioration**.

### Orders

Orders declined by only:

**0.3% YoY**

This is effectively stable compared with the magnitude of the demand decline.

### Demand per Order

Demand per order declined by:

**37.3% YoY**

This is the central KPI explaining the performance deterioration.

---

# 4. Root Cause: Product Volume Contraction

The most important analytical finding is that the decline is visible across the product portfolio.

The number of products remained:

**20 in 2018 → 20 in 2019**

Therefore, the decline cannot be attributed to a smaller product portfolio.

Instead, the amount of demand generated by the portfolio declined substantially.

Average demand per product fell from approximately:

**41.0M → 25.1M units**

representing:

**-38.7% YoY**

This is almost identical to the decline in demand per order.

### Interpretation

The business still has broadly the same product range, but those products are generating significantly less volume.

This establishes **product demand contraction as the primary driver of the overall volume decline**.

The 2019 Top 5 contains:

* Videoright
* Storsql
* Sociplace

**44M and 40M units**

For example:

**Whathtype → ~82M**

versus:

**Shareit → ~44M**

**Streamer → ~64M**

versus:

**Publishit → ~40M**

Therefore, the movement in rankings does not represent successful product replacement.

### The business has experienced:

**Loss of high-volume demand**

↓

**Lower-volume products move up the rankings**

↓

**Replacement volume is insufficient**

↓

**Total demand declines**

↓

**Demand per order declines**

This is the clearest causal chain supported by the dashboard evidence.

---

# 8. Pricing Is Not the Primary Cause

Value per unit increased:

**$357.74 → $370.06**

or:

**+3.4%**

This is important because it rules out a major pricing/value deterioration as the principal explanation.

The business is generating slightly more value from each unit while simultaneously selling substantially fewer units.

Therefore:

> **The primary problem is volume, not unit value.**

Management should therefore avoid responding to the problem primarily through price reductions.

Reducing prices without restoring demand could worsen profitability without solving the underlying issue.

---

# 9. Warehouse Concentration

Cedar Park is the dominant warehouse.

It contributes approximately:

* **68% of demand**
* **69% of revenue**

with approximately **$156.67bn** in reported revenue.

Berkeley contributes approximately **26%**, while Montcalm and Oakland Mills contribute considerably less.

## Decision implication

Cedar Park represents the largest operational concentration point in the network.

Any:

* capacity constraint,
* inventory issue,
* staffing problem,
* equipment problem,
* service disruption,

at Cedar Park could have a disproportionately large impact on overall business performance.

### Recommendation

Management should conduct a detailed Cedar Park performance review covering:

* capacity;
* throughput;
* inventory availability;
* staffing;
* equipment;
* operational downtime;
* demand versus capacity.

---

# 10. Smaller Warehouse Performance

Montcalm and Oakland Mills contribute relatively small portions of total revenue.

This creates a potential efficiency question.

However, the available dashboard does **not** provide sufficient information to conclude that these warehouses should be closed or consolidated.

Before making such a decision, management should evaluate:

* capacity utilisation;
* operating cost;
* demand handled;
* units per labour hour;
* cost per unit;
* contribution margin;
* inventory holding cost.

### Decision rule

Do not make a warehouse closure decision based on revenue contribution alone.

First determine whether the smaller warehouses are:

**underutilised, strategically necessary, or operationally efficient despite their lower volume.**

---

# 11. Category Concentration

WorkSuite 1000 represents approximately **77% of demand** based on the category analysis.

This creates significant category concentration.

The business is therefore exposed to a major risk:

> **A sustained decline in WorkSuite 1000 demand could materially affect total business performance.**

Management should therefore:

1. Protect the existing WorkSuite 1000 volume.
2. Identify the reason for its performance.
3. Assess the growth potential of Bi Stack and Tbox.
4. Develop profitable demand diversification.

The objective should not be to reduce WorkSuite 1000 dependency at the expense of existing revenue.

The objective should be to **build additional demand drivers around it**.

---

**73M in January**

↓

**43M in February**

↓

**69M in March**

↓

**Low-to-mid 40M range later in the year**

* product-specific demand changes;
* warehouse constraints;
* changes in purchasing patterns.

The monthly pattern should be used as an **early-warning indicator** going forward.

---

# 13. What the Business Should NOT Do

Based on the evidence, management should avoid several premature responses.

### Do not simply pursue more orders

Orders are already broadly stable.

Increasing order count without restoring order volume would not necessarily solve the underlying problem.

### Do not immediately cut prices

Value per unit increased 3.4%, while volume fell 37.5%.

The evidence does not support pricing as the primary problem.

### Do not immediately close smaller warehouses

Their lower contribution identifies a question, not a conclusion.

Capacity and cost data are required before making a structural decision.

### Do not interpret new Top 5 products as successful replacement

The replacement products are generating significantly lower volumes than the high-volume products they displaced.

---

# 14. Recommended Decision Actions

## Priority 1 — Recover High-Volume Product Demand

Focus immediate commercial and operational investigation on products with the largest absolute volume losses.

Particular attention should be given to:

* Whathtype
* Streamer
* other products showing significant YoY volume contraction

### Decision

Determine whether these products require:

* demand-generation activity;
* inventory intervention;
* pricing review;
* product repositioning;
* operational support;
* customer/order investigation where additional data becomes available.

---

# 15. Priority 2 — Build a Product Recovery Programme

Create a product-level recovery matrix:

| Product Group           | Action               |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| High volume / declining | Immediate recovery   |
| High volume / stable    | Protect              |
| Growing products        | Invest               |
| Low volume / declining  | Review               |
| Low volume / stable     | Maintain selectively |

This ensures resources are directed toward products capable of materially affecting total demand.

---

# 16. Priority 3 — Investigate the Drivers Behind Whathtype and Streamer

The approximate **93M combined unit loss** makes these products the first candidates for root-cause investigation.

Management should examine:

* historical monthly demand;
* warehouse distribution;
* category relationship;
* availability;
* operational constraints;
* pricing/value;
* product lifecycle;
* seasonality.

The objective is to determine why products that previously generated very high volumes experienced such significant contraction.

---

# 17. Priority 4 — Protect High-Volume Product Capacity

The business should ensure that the warehouses supporting its major volume drivers have sufficient:

* inventory;
* labour;
* storage capacity;
* equipment;
* throughput capability.

This is particularly important for Cedar Park because of its approximately **68% demand contribution**.

---

# 18. Priority 5 — Diversify Future Growth

The business should develop secondary demand drivers.

WorkSuite 1000 currently dominates demand.

Management should identify products/categories capable of growing enough to provide meaningful additional volume.

This reduces the risk associated with relying heavily on a small number of demand drivers.

# 22. Final Business Conclusion

The evidence provides a clear explanation for the decline in units per order.

> **The business experienced a major contraction in product demand while order volume remained virtually unchanged. The decline was particularly severe among historically high-volume products, causing the upper end of the product portfolio to fall from approximately 61M–82M units in 2018 to 40M–49M units in 2019. Products moving into the leading positions were unable to replace the volume lost from the previous high-volume products. As a result, substantially less product volume was generated across approximately the same number of orders, producing the 37.3% decline in demand per order.**

This makes the strategic problem clear:

**The business does not primarily need more transactions. It needs to recover lost volume.**

The most immediate opportunity is therefore to **recover demand from historically high-volume products, beginning with Whathtype and Streamer, while protecting existing high-volume products and reducing dependency on a small number of warehouses and categories.**
